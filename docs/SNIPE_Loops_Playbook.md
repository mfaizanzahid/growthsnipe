# SNIPE Loops

## A practical revenue playbook for home-service businesses

**Turn more inquiries into booked work. Make every customer experience count. Build relationships that bring customers back.**

This guide gives you eight practical ways to improve the customer journey, increase the value of each job, encourage repeat business, and strengthen referral relationships. Each loop is adapted for home inspection, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, and general contracting businesses.


# How to Use This Guide

Start with the chapter for your trade. Choose one loop that addresses a current business need, then use the steps as a starting point. You do not need to introduce every loop at once.

Each loop includes four simple parts:

- **When to start:** the customer or business event that makes the loop relevant.
- **What to do:** the calls, messages, or follow-up actions to take.
- **Keep in mind:** details that help the experience stay useful and professional.
- **When to stop / What to track:** when the follow-up should end and how to judge the result.

## A Few Simple Guidelines

- Make every message relevant to the customer's service, property, equipment, or project.
- Use accurate appointment details, prices, service descriptions, and warranty dates.
- Offer optional services clearly. Never make a customer feel that an optional add-on is required.
- Respect communication preferences, stop when someone asks you to stop, and send technical, safety, complaint, or warranty questions to someone qualified to answer them.

## What Happens at Each Stage?

| Customer or partner moment | Best next step |
|---|---|
| A new inquiry arrives | Acknowledge it quickly, make contact, and help the customer take the next step. |
| The customer is ready to book | Present relevant packages or add-ons and record the customer's choice. |
| The appointment is coming up | Confirm the details, share preparation instructions, and offer a suitable extra if appropriate. |
| The job is complete | Send the promised report or job information, invite feedback, and make it easy to ask for help. |
| The customer may need another service | Choose the most relevant follow-up: cross-sell, warranty reminder, annual checkup, or repeat test. |
| A referral partner sends work or becomes inactive | Thank them, recognize meaningful milestones, stay in touch, and reconnect personally when needed. |


# The Eight Revenue Loops

The same eight loops can work across different trades, but the service, timing, and customer message should change. The trade chapters that follow show how to apply each one.

## Loop 1 - Booking-Cart Upsell

**Purpose:** Increase the value of a booking by offering a useful package or additional service while the customer is already deciding what to book.

**In practice:** Explain the main service first. Then offer relevant options, explain what each includes, show the price clearly, and let the customer choose. Record what was accepted or declined so the same offer is not repeated unnecessarily.

## Loop 2 - Pre-Service Add-On

**Purpose:** Help the customer prepare for the visit and offer a relevant extra that can still be completed at the same time.

**In practice:** Confirm the date, time, location, access details, and any preparation needed. Offer one suitable extra only when it is available and can be added without disrupting the appointment.

## Loop 3 - Delivery and Reputation

**Purpose:** Give the customer a clear handover, collect useful feedback, resolve problems, and invite honest reviews.

**In practice:** Send the report, job summary, photos, invoice, or project documents when they are ready. Include a clear contact route for questions. Ask for feedback after the customer has had a reasonable chance to review the work.

## Loop 4 - Post-Service Cross-Sell

**Purpose:** Help the customer with another relevant service after the original job.

**In practice:** Use the customer's service history and property, equipment, or project details to choose one useful next offer. Start with helpful information, then make the next step easy.

## Loop 5 - Warranty Lifecycle

**Purpose:** Contact customers before a real warranty milestone or expiry date.

**In practice:** Use the actual warranty information. Remind the customer of the recorded date and offer an appropriate service where it makes sense. Refer coverage or claims questions to the warranty provider or a qualified person.

## Loop 6 - Annual Checkup

**Purpose:** Create a useful reason to reconnect for periodic maintenance or a condition check.

**In practice:** Use the customer's service history, maintenance plan, or equipment guidance to decide when a check is appropriate. Explain what the visit includes and make booking straightforward.

## Loop 7 - Re-Test Cycle

**Purpose:** Remind customers when a previous test or assessment may be due again.

**In practice:** Use the last test date and the relevant guidance to set the next reminder. Explain why a repeat may be useful, and update the next date after the new test is completed.

## Loop 8 - Partner Reactivation

**Purpose:** Build lasting relationships with the people and businesses that refer work to you.

**In practice:** Thank partners for their first referral, recognize repeat business, stay in touch with useful updates, and make a personal effort to reconnect when referrals slow down.


# Trade-Specific Revenue Loops

Choose the chapter that matches your business. Each chapter shows when to use each loop, what to do, what to watch out for, and what results to track. Adapt the examples to the services you actually provide.


## Home Inspection

The home-inspection journey is driven by the booking, the property, the inspection/report handover, additional testing, homeowner milestones, and real estate referral relationships.

### Loop 1 - Booking-Cart Upsell

**When to start:** A new inspection quote or booking is being set and the property details and requested inspection type are known.

**What to do:**


1. Present the core inspection and explain its scope.

2. Offer appropriate additional services such as radon testing, sewer scope, WDO/termite inspection, pool or well-related services only where offered and relevant to the property.

3. Record accept/decline against the order, update the ticket, and notify the team if an accepted service needs a separate technician or schedule.


**Keep in mind:** Use property age, foundation type, local service availability, and customer request only when reliable. Do not imply a defect exists or recommend a test as mandatory unless a valid requirement supports that statement.

**When to stop / What to track:** Booking confirmed, add-on decision recorded, or customer exits the booking flow. Track how often each additional service is selected, the average inspection price, and the extra revenue per inspection.

### Loop 2 - Pre-Service Add-On

**When to start:** An inspection is confirmed and the appointment is approximately 24-48 hours away; the order remains active.

**What to do:**


1. Send the actual appointment time/window, property address, access instructions, and attendance/preparation information.

2. Offer one relevant add-on that was not selected at booking, such as radon, sewer scope, or WDO where appropriate and still schedulable.

3. Update the order and inspection team if the customer accepts; otherwise send the final reminder using the current appointment data.


**Keep in mind:** Do not promote an add-on already accepted or declined for this booking. Re-check cancellation, reschedule, property access, and technician availability before every send.

**When to stop / What to track:** Inspection completed, cancelled, rescheduled outside the window, or add-on decision recorded. Track add-on conversion, confirmation rate, and reschedule/no-show rate.

### Loop 3 - Delivery and Reputation

**When to start:** Inspection status is Completed and the inspection report is published or otherwise confirmed ready for delivery.

**What to do:**


1. Send the report link and explain how the buyer or homeowner can ask a question.

2. Where your company provides it, include an agent-facing repair-request or report-sharing resource and clarify the next step.

3. After the customer has had time to review the report, request feedback and invite an honest public review; route complaints or report questions to the inspection team.


**Keep in mind:** Do not tell the customer the report is ready until it is actually published. Pause generic review and promotional messages while a service issue is actively being handled.

**When to stop / What to track:** Report delivered, issue resolved, and review invitation sent. Track time-to-report, customer feedback, review volume, and service-recovery time.

### Loop 4 - Post-Service Cross-Sell

**When to start:** A completed inspection reaches a set post-close or move-in period, commonly around 30 days, or a verified property event creates a relevant need.

**What to do:**


1. Group customers by property details and the services they have already purchased.

2. Send useful homeowner education based on relevant property characteristics, such as age-related maintenance considerations.

3. Offer one suitable follow-on service, for example indoor-air-quality or mold-related service where offered, and route questions to a knowledgeable person.


**Keep in mind:** Do not send every homeowner the same list of services. Do not offer services the customer has already purchased. Skip customers for whom the service is not relevant, anyone who has opted out, and anyone with an unresolved complaint.

**When to stop / What to track:** Service booked, customer declines, or the offer expires. Track cross-sell bookings, revenue per inspected property, and unsubscribe/complaint rate.

### Loop 5 - Warranty Lifecycle

**When to start:** A documented builder-warranty milestone is approaching; a homeowner may be contacted around months 9 and 10 when that timing matches the documented builder warranty.

**What to do:**


1. Use the verified closing date and the relevant warranty milestone to calculate outreach.

2. Explain that the homeowner may wish to consider a pre-expiry inspection and what the inspection can help document.

3. Offer a booking link and route questions about warranty rights, coverage, or deadlines to the appropriate qualified contact.


**Keep in mind:** Never infer the warranty length from the closing date alone. Use the builder's or warranty provider's actual documents, and refer questions about coverage or legal deadlines to the appropriate qualified contact.

**When to stop / What to track:** Customer books, the documented window passes, or the date record is corrected. Track pre-expiry bookings and completeness of closing/warranty dates.

### Loop 6 - Annual Checkup

**When to start:** The property reaches a business-defined 12- or 24-month follow-up point after the original inspection, or a relevant seasonal campaign is scheduled.

**What to do:**


1. Send a homeowner check-in with one practical maintenance or property-care tip.

2. Explain the scope of your company's home-health checkup or other available follow-up service.

3. Offer a booking route and, where useful, keep the homeowner in occasional helpful homeowner updates.


**Keep in mind:** Be clear that a general home checkup is not necessarily a full resale inspection or a substitute for specialist assessment. Avoid repeating the offer after a decline.

**When to stop / What to track:** New checkup booked or the contact returns to occasional helpful updates the customer has agreed to receive. Track 12/24-month rebooking, repeat revenue, and email engagement alongside actual bookings.

### Loop 7 - Re-Test Cycle

**When to start:** A recorded radon test or another additional test reaches the agreed follow-up date; a 24-month radon reminder may be a useful starting point, but the right timing depends on the applicable guidance and the customer's test history.

**What to do:**


1. Store the prior test type, property, completion date, and approved next-due rule.

2. Send an educational reminder that references the prior test and why a new test may be considered.

3. Provide a booking path and update the next-due date only when a new test is completed.


**Keep in mind:** Use the actual test history and applicable guidance. Do not treat 24 months as a universal interval for every test or every jurisdiction.

**When to stop / What to track:** Test booked and completed, customer declines, or the due window passes. Track re-test bookings, completion rate, and accuracy of prior-test records.

### Loop 8 - Partner Reactivation

**When to start:** A referral agent or other partner sends a first, repeat, milestone, or top-tier completed inspection, or their referral activity falls below the normal cadence.

**What to do:**


1. Recognize the first and second completed inspection with personal appreciation; acknowledge repeat milestones such as 3, 5, 7, and 9, and VIP status at 10+ if that tier fits your business.

2. Keep active agents connected with useful report/process updates, education, and quarterly relationship touches; use a handwritten note or birthday greeting only when the information is supplied and appropriate.

3. Set a reminder when partner referrals slow down around 60 days without a booking, then use a personal reactivation sequence around 90 days or your normal referral cycle; welcome the agent back when a completed inspection returns.


**Keep in mind:** Base milestone counts on completed inspections linked to the agent. Do not send a generic dormant message to a currently active/top-tier agent. Use your real referral cadence to set thresholds.

**When to stop / What to track:** Partner resumes sending work or remains in an occasional, relevant partner updates. Track active agents, completed inspections per agent, partner concentration, inactivity recovery, and win-back rate.


## HVAC

The HVAC journey is driven by the service request, system type and age, diagnostic or maintenance history, equipment warranties, seasonal demand, and relationships with property managers, builders, and other local partners.

### Loop 1 - Booking-Cart Upsell

**When to start:** A service request or estimate is being set and the system type, customer concern, and known equipment details are available.

**What to do:**


1. Offer clear service levels or good-better-best options where your business has defined scope and pricing.

2. Present only relevant additions such as an indoor-air-quality assessment, duct-related service, thermostat work, or a maintenance-plan option when appropriate.

3. Record selected and declined options on the work order and make sure the booked scope matches what the technician is prepared to deliver.


**Keep in mind:** Do not use equipment age alone to claim failure or imminent replacement. Do not bundle services that your business cannot perform or explain.

**When to stop / What to track:** Customer confirms the booking or quote scope. Track package mix, add-on share of bookings that include the add-on, average ticket, and gross margin by job type.

### Loop 2 - Pre-Service Add-On

**When to start:** A maintenance, diagnostic, repair, or installation visit is scheduled and the visit remains active.

**What to do:**


1. Confirm appointment window, service address, system type if known, access to the equipment, and any relevant preparation.

2. Offer one suitable additional service that can be performed during the planned visit, such as an agreed maintenance-plan enrollment or related assessment.

3. Update the work order and dispatch/team notes if accepted; send the final reminder with the current visit details.


**Keep in mind:** Check technician capability, parts, time allocation, and customer permission before adding work. Never ask customers to open equipment or perform technical tasks.

**When to stop / What to track:** Visit completed or rescheduled, or add-on decision recorded. Track pre-visit conversion, schedule adherence, and additional work completed per visit.

### Loop 3 - Delivery and Reputation

**When to start:** The work order is marked Completed and the technician's job summary and invoice or service documentation are ready.

**What to do:**


1. Send a concise summary of work performed, relevant photos or readings where available, and any customer instructions approved by the technician.

2. Explain the next step for payment, warranty documents, or questions using the actual account status.

3. After the customer has had a reasonable opportunity to experience the service, request honest feedback and a review; route callbacks and unresolved comfort issues to service staff.


**Keep in mind:** Suppress generic review requests when there is an open callback, repeat visit, safety concern, or unresolved complaint.

**When to stop / What to track:** Handover delivered and open issues assigned. Track callbacks, first-time fix where available, issue resolution, review volume, and repeat service rate.

### Loop 4 - Post-Service Cross-Sell

**When to start:** A completed repair or installation reaches a defined follow-up window, or a recorded customer/system need makes another service relevant.

**What to do:**


1. Use the equipment and service history to choose one relevant next step, such as a maintenance agreement, air-quality assessment, or related system service.

2. Send a short educational message that explains the purpose and any customer benefit without promising unverified energy savings.

3. Provide a booking or reply route and hand technical questions to the service team.


**Keep in mind:** Do not send a replacement or upgrade pitch simply because the unit is old. Respect current service-plan enrollment and existing appointments.

**When to stop / What to track:** Offer booked, declined, or moved to lower-frequency customer education. Track repeat booking, maintenance-plan conversion, and revenue per customer.

### Loop 5 - Warranty Lifecycle

**When to start:** A verified manufacturer, parts, labor, or workmanship warranty milestone is approaching.

**What to do:**


1. Read the warranty type and relevant start/end date from the installation or equipment record.

2. Send a reminder that identifies the equipment or work and the actual recorded date; explain any documented service or registration step only when confirmed.

3. Offer a qualified service appointment where useful and route coverage or claim questions to staff or the warranty provider.


**Keep in mind:** Do not invent warranty duration or imply that service is required to preserve coverage unless the manufacturer's actual terms support it.

**When to stop / What to track:** Warranty-related appointment booked or milestone passed. Track warranty data completeness, engagement, and completed warranty-window service.

### Loop 6 - Annual Checkup

**When to start:** A seasonal or annual maintenance date from the customer's plan, equipment record, or approved service schedule is due.

**What to do:**


1. Use the service plan or manufacturer guidance to identify the appropriate maintenance period.

2. Send a seasonal reminder that explains what the visit covers and offers a convenient booking route.

3. After the visit, update the last-service date and next due date in the customer/equipment record.


**Keep in mind:** Do not assume a single annual interval suits all systems. Where your business sells twice-yearly seasonal visits, use the actual plan and local operating model.

**When to stop / What to track:** Visit booked and completed or customer declines. Track plan renewal, seasonal booking rate, repeat visits, and completion against scheduled capacity.

### Loop 7 - Re-Test Cycle

**When to start:** A documented equipment check, air-quality test, measurement, or prior diagnostic reaches its business-approved recheck date.

**What to do:**


1. Identify the exact previous test or measurement and its recorded result/date.

2. Send a reminder describing the reason for a follow-up and the service your company can actually perform.

3. Book the technician and update the next-due record only after the work is completed.


**Keep in mind:** Do not invent universal filter, refrigerant, or system-testing intervals. Follow equipment documentation, applicable requirements, and your company's approved maintenance policy.

**When to stop / What to track:** Recheck booked/completed or dismissed by a human reviewer. Track share of reminders that lead to bookings, completion, and the quality of equipment history.

### Loop 8 - Partner Reactivation

**When to start:** A property manager, builder, realtor, facility contact, or complementary trade partner sends work or becomes inactive.

**What to do:**


1. Thank a partner after the first completed referred job; acknowledge repeat work and meaningful annual or volume milestones.

2. Keep active partners informed with useful scheduling, service-area, emergency-routing, and maintenance-program updates rather than generic promotions.

3. Flag partner inactivity after a period based on normal referral frequency, then make a personal call or email to ask what would make coordination easier; welcome back the next completed referral.


**Keep in mind:** Make sure each job is linked to the partner who referred it, and distinguish a seasonal pause from a lost relationship. Do not promise preferential treatment that conflicts with capacity or policy.

**When to stop / What to track:** Partner becomes active again or remains in appropriate occasional helpful partner updates. Track active partners, revenue from referrals, partner response, and win-back rate.


## Plumbing

The plumbing journey is driven by the reported problem, urgency, property and system context, estimate approval, installed products, water/drainage service history, and relationships with property managers and construction partners.

### Loop 1 - Booking-Cart Upsell

**When to start:** A plumbing inquiry is scoped or a quote is being prepared and the issue, fixture/system, and requested work are understood.

**What to do:**


1. Confirm the customer's stated problem and the service needed to address it.

2. Offer related options only when relevant, such as a drain/sewer camera inspection, water-heater assessment, leak detection, water-quality service, or fixture replacement.

3. Show separate scope and pricing, record the choice, and ensure the technician and work order reflect the confirmed work.


**Keep in mind:** Never use fear-based language or claim hidden damage without evidence. Emergency or active-leak inquiries should be triaged before promotional add-ons.

**When to stop / What to track:** Scope accepted and booking confirmed. Track related-service share of bookings that include the add-on, average ticket, estimate acceptance, and margin.

### Loop 2 - Pre-Service Add-On

**When to start:** A plumbing appointment is confirmed and the visit is approaching.

**What to do:**


1. Confirm appointment time, affected fixture or system, access instructions, parking or property-entry notes, and any agreed preparation.

2. Offer one related service that can reasonably be completed during the same visit, if the customer has not already selected it.

3. Update the work order and technician notes when accepted and send the final reminder with accurate appointment details.


**Keep in mind:** Do not ask customers to dismantle plumbing, handle hazardous materials, or attempt repairs. If the issue becomes urgent or changes, route it to a person for triage.

**When to stop / What to track:** Appointment completed, changed, or add-on accepted/declined. Track pre-visit add-ons, missed appointments, and technician scope changes.

### Loop 3 - Delivery and Reputation

**When to start:** The job is marked Completed and the work summary, photos, invoice, or required customer documents are ready.

**What to do:**


1. Send the work completed, parts or fixtures installed where recorded, and any approved care or usage instructions.

2. Provide the correct next step for payment, warranty documentation, or questions.

3. Request honest feedback and a review after the customer has had an opportunity to assess the service; route unresolved leaks, callbacks, or complaints to the service manager.


**Keep in mind:** Do not close the customer journey while a leak, return visit, or customer complaint is still active. Technical aftercare instructions must be approved by the plumber.

**When to stop / What to track:** Handover delivered and exceptions assigned. Track repeat visits, callback rate, complaint resolution, and review volume.

### Loop 4 - Post-Service Cross-Sell

**When to start:** A completed service reaches a relevant follow-up window or the customer record shows a related need.

**What to do:**


1. Group customers by prior service: water heater, drain, fixture, leak, water treatment, or other recorded work.

2. Send practical education relevant to that service and offer one complementary service where your company has a defined offering.

3. Give the customer a clear way to ask a question or schedule a visit.


**Keep in mind:** Do not imply that another plumbing issue exists without evidence. Suppress already purchased services and avoid repeated generic offers.

**When to stop / What to track:** Customer books, declines, or remains in occasional helpful updates the customer has agreed to receive. Track repeat booking, related-service booking rate, and customer revenue.

### Loop 5 - Warranty Lifecycle

**When to start:** A product, equipment, parts, or workmanship warranty record reaches a defined pre-expiry window.

**What to do:**


1. Use the recorded product or installation details and warranty dates.

2. Send a reminder naming the item or work and the documented date; explain only verified maintenance or registration steps.

3. Offer a qualified service visit when relevant and route coverage questions to the manufacturer, installer, or responsible team member.


**Keep in mind:** Do not infer the length or scope of a warranty from a generic product category. Warranty claims and interpretations require a person.

**When to stop / What to track:** Customer requests service or the date passes. Track warranty-related bookings, completed work, and warranty record completeness.

### Loop 6 - Annual Checkup

**When to start:** A customer reaches an annual or business-defined periodic check date, or an installed system's approved service plan indicates a visit is due.

**What to do:**


1. Use actual installation and service records to identify whether a check is appropriate.

2. Send a short reminder explaining the specific check offered, such as a water-system review or a maintenance visit for a relevant installed product.

3. Book the visit and update the service history and next planned check.


**Keep in mind:** Do not promote a blanket annual plumbing inspection unless your company has defined what it includes and why it is appropriate for that customer.

**When to stop / What to track:** Visit booked or customer continues to receive occasional, relevant updates. Track periodic service bookings, plan retention where offered, and repeat customer revenue.

### Loop 7 - Re-Test Cycle

**When to start:** A prior water-quality test, drain/sewer assessment, or other repeatable plumbing test reaches its documented follow-up date.

**What to do:**


1. Retrieve the prior service type, result/date where available, and property or system link.

2. Send a reminder describing the prior test and the reason a repeat may be appropriate.

3. Offer a new appointment and record the next due date only after the new test or assessment is completed.


**Keep in mind:** Use your approved policy, applicable requirements, and equipment guidance. Do not invent a universal retest interval.

**When to stop / What to track:** Repeat test completed or closed. Track share of reminders that lead to bookings, completed repeat services, and accurate prior-test history.

### Loop 8 - Partner Reactivation

**When to start:** A property manager, real estate agent, builder, restoration company, or other approved referral partner sends a completed job or becomes inactive.

**What to do:**


1. Recognize the first completed referral and subsequent work milestones with a personal thank-you.

2. Keep active partners informed with clear service-area, response, scheduling, documentation, and after-hours escalation information.

3. When referrals fall below the partner's usual pattern, reach out personally and ask what has changed before offering a generic incentive.


**Keep in mind:** Avoid disclosing customer details to partners without permission. Link each referred job to the correct partner and distinguish seasonal inactivity from a lost relationship.

**When to stop / What to track:** Partner sends another job or remains in occasional helpful partner updates. Track active partners, referred jobs, response/coordination quality, and recovered relationships.


## Electrical

The electrical journey is driven by the customer's stated need, site and panel context, approved scope, equipment installed, required documentation, and relationships with builders, property managers, and complementary trades.

### Loop 1 - Booking-Cart Upsell

**When to start:** An electrical inquiry is being scoped or quoted and the customer's requested work and known site details are available.

**What to do:**


1. Clarify the core electrical job and the intended outcome.

2. Offer only relevant options within your company's qualified scope, such as a panel assessment, surge protection, EV-charger consultation, generator-related service, or additional devices.

3. Separate optional work and pricing in the estimate, then record acceptance and update the work order.


**Keep in mind:** Do not imply a safety defect or code violation without an appropriate assessment. Never make a customer perform electrical checks or open a panel to qualify for an offer.

**When to stop / What to track:** Approved scope and booking confirmed. Track optional-service share of bookings that include the add-on, average ticket, estimate acceptance, and job margin.

### Loop 2 - Pre-Service Add-On

**When to start:** A site visit, installation, or repair is scheduled and the appointment remains active.

**What to do:**


1. Confirm arrival window, property access, decision-maker availability, and any site details the technician has asked the customer to provide.

2. Offer a relevant related service only if it can be safely scoped and scheduled during the visit.

3. Update the work order and send the final confirmation using the current appointment details.


**Keep in mind:** Do not request that customers handle wiring, open panels, test circuits, or perform other electrical work. Questions affecting safety must be routed to a qualified person.

**When to stop / What to track:** Visit completed or rescheduled and scope updated. Track visit readiness, accepted add-ons, reschedules, and return visits caused by missing site information.

### Loop 3 - Delivery and Reputation

**When to start:** The work order is Completed and approved documentation is ready for customer handover.

**What to do:**


1. Send the completed-work summary and applicable photos, manuals, permits, inspection documentation, or device information where available.

2. Explain the contact path for questions, follow-up, or a future service request.

3. After the work is complete and any immediate issue is addressed, invite honest feedback and a review.


**Keep in mind:** Only send documents that are final and approved for customer release. Escalate safety-related feedback and unresolved faults immediately.

**When to stop / What to track:** Handover delivered and any issue assigned. Track documentation completeness, callbacks, issue resolution, and review volume.

### Loop 4 - Post-Service Cross-Sell

**When to start:** A completed electrical job reaches a suitable follow-up window or the customer has a recorded related project need.

**What to do:**


1. Use the installed system/device and original scope to identify one reasonable next service.

2. Send educational content about the relevant option, such as a future EV-charger project, surge protection, generator consultation, or another service actually offered.

3. Invite the customer to request a consultation or estimate without implying that the current installation is inadequate.


**Keep in mind:** Suppress purchased or declined services and avoid technical recommendations that are not supported by site assessment.

**When to stop / What to track:** Consultation or estimate booked, or customer declines. Track repeat projects, consultation conversion, and revenue per customer.

### Loop 5 - Warranty Lifecycle

**When to start:** The recorded warranty for installed equipment, parts, or workmanship approaches a verified milestone.

**What to do:**


1. Read the actual product, installation, and warranty record.

2. Send a reminder identifying the covered item and recorded date, with any confirmed service or documentation action.

3. Offer a qualified appointment if useful and route coverage or claim questions to the manufacturer or responsible team member.


**Keep in mind:** Do not promise coverage or state that the customer must purchase service to retain a warranty unless that requirement is verified in the applicable terms.

**When to stop / What to track:** Warranty inquiry or service booked, or milestone passes. Track warranty-date accuracy and completed follow-up work.

### Loop 6 - Annual Checkup

**When to start:** A customer-specific maintenance plan or approved periodic check date is reached.

**What to do:**


1. Use the actual installation, device, and service history to determine whether a check or consultation is appropriate.

2. Describe the scope of the offered visit and the qualified personnel who will perform it.

3. Provide a booking option and update the record after the visit.


**Keep in mind:** Do not market an annual electrical inspection as universally necessary or imply compliance certification without a defined inspection scope and appropriate qualifications.

**When to stop / What to track:** Visit or consultation booked/completed. Track plan retention, repeat work, and customer-specific follow-up completion.

### Loop 7 - Re-Test Cycle

**When to start:** A recorded device, system, or site assessment reaches a follow-up date based on the applicable manufacturer guidance, project record, or approved service policy.

**What to do:**


1. Identify the prior device/system and the exact assessment or test performed.

2. Send a reminder that describes the recorded service and offers a qualified follow-up where warranted.

3. Book with the appropriate technician and update the next due date only after work is completed.


**Keep in mind:** Do not infer a testing interval or provide do-it-yourself electrical testing guidance. Any safety concern must be handed to a qualified electrician.

**When to stop / What to track:** Follow-up assessment booked/completed. Track share of reminders that lead to bookings and completeness of device/service records.

### Loop 8 - Partner Reactivation

**When to start:** A builder, general contractor, property manager, solar installer, HVAC company, or other approved partner sends work or becomes inactive.

**What to do:**


1. Thank the partner after the first completed referred job and recognize repeat work or agreed annual milestones.

2. Support active partners with clear scope boundaries, scheduling expectations, documentation handoffs, and contact routes.

3. Create a reminder when partner referrals slow down when normal referral activity stops; reconnect personally to understand upcoming projects, service gaps, or coordination issues.


**Keep in mind:** Keep referral arrangements transparent and appropriate. Do not promise priority response or pricing outside approved business policy.

**When to stop / What to track:** Partner returns with a job or continues to receive occasional, relevant updates. Track partner-sourced work, active partner count, repeat frequency, and recovered referrals.


## Roofing

The roofing journey is driven by inspection or estimate requests, roof condition and age, weather events, project scope, material and workmanship records, and relationships with property managers, builders, real estate professionals, and restoration partners.

### Loop 1 - Booking-Cart Upsell

**When to start:** A roof inspection, repair request, or replacement estimate is being scoped and the roof/property details are known.

**What to do:**


1. Clarify the requested work and what the initial visit or estimate includes.

2. Offer relevant scope options such as gutter assessment, flashing review, ventilation assessment, or related exterior work when supported by the inspection and company capability.

3. Separate necessary work from optional improvements and record the customer's decision in the estimate.


**Keep in mind:** Do not claim storm damage, leaks, or hidden defects without documented evidence. Keep repair recommendations and optional upgrades distinguishable.

**When to stop / What to track:** Estimate or booking confirmed. Track scope acceptance, average project value, share of bookings that include the add-on for related work, and gross margin.

### Loop 2 - Pre-Service Add-On

**When to start:** A roof inspection, repair, or project start is scheduled and weather and access conditions can be reviewed.

**What to do:**


1. Confirm site access, arrival window, decision-maker availability, pets/parking or other relevant access notes, and weather contingency.

2. Identify any related work that can be included without delaying or confusing the agreed scope.

3. Confirm the work area and documentation needed, then send a final reminder with the current schedule and contact route.


**Keep in mind:** Re-check weather, crew capacity, materials, and access before sending. Do not promise a start date that depends on unconfirmed conditions.

**When to stop / What to track:** Site visit or project proceeds, reschedules, or scope is updated. Track readiness, weather-related reschedules, and approved scope additions.

### Loop 3 - Delivery and Reputation

**When to start:** The repair or project reaches a verified completion/handover milestone and photos, inspection notes, and customer documents are ready.

**What to do:**


1. Send a completion summary with relevant before/after photos, work performed, and any agreed exclusions or remaining items.

2. Provide care instructions, invoice information, and applicable warranty documents where available.

3. After punch-list issues are resolved or assigned, invite honest customer feedback and a review.


**Keep in mind:** Do not mark the project as fully complete when open punch-list items remain. Route water intrusion, workmanship concerns, and complaints to the project owner.

**When to stop / What to track:** Handover completed and outstanding items assigned. Track punch-list closure, callback rate, review volume, and time to resolve issues.

### Loop 4 - Post-Service Cross-Sell

**When to start:** A completed roof job reaches a set post-project period or the customer experiences a relevant seasonal/weather event.

**What to do:**


1. Use the roof and project history to select one relevant follow-on service, such as gutter maintenance, a separate exterior assessment, or another documented need.

2. Send a seasonal education message tied to local weather or the customer's actual roof system.

3. Offer a site assessment or estimate when appropriate, without implying new damage.


**Keep in mind:** Suppress work already included in the original scope and avoid fear-based weather or damage claims.

**When to stop / What to track:** Follow-on assessment or work booked. Track repeat project rate, related-service booking rate, and revenue per property.

### Loop 5 - Warranty Lifecycle

**When to start:** A documented workmanship or manufacturer/material warranty reaches a pre-expiry window or an agreed check milestone.

**What to do:**


1. Use the actual completion date, warranty type, product/material details, and recorded terms.

2. Send a reminder that identifies the warranty record and offers a qualified inspection or review where appropriate.

3. Route coverage, claim, or interpretation questions to the responsible project manager or warranty provider.


**Keep in mind:** Do not assume every roofing warranty has the same term or coverage. Do not promise a claim outcome.

**When to stop / What to track:** Customer books a review or the milestone passes. Track warranty-window checks and accuracy of warranty documentation.

### Loop 6 - Annual Checkup

**When to start:** The property reaches a business-defined roof/exterior check window or a seasonal campaign is appropriate for that customer.

**What to do:**


1. Use prior inspection findings, roof age if known, repair history, and service area to determine fit.

2. Offer a roof, gutter, flashing, or exterior condition check with a clear scope.

3. Book the visit and record findings as a new service event rather than assuming the previous condition remains unchanged.


**Keep in mind:** Do not present annual inspection as mandatory for all roofs. Timing and scope should reflect the property, local conditions, and company policy.

**When to stop / What to track:** Check booked/completed or customer declines. Track repeat assessment rate and follow-on work supported by documented findings.

### Loop 7 - Re-Test Cycle

**When to start:** A prior roof assessment, repair follow-up, storm-related inspection, or documented condition reaches a review date.

**What to do:**


1. Link the prior inspection/repair record to the property and record any recommended follow-up date.

2. When the due date or a relevant event occurs, send a factual reminder referencing the prior visit or event.

3. Offer a new assessment and update the next follow-up only after the visit is completed and findings are documented.


**Keep in mind:** Do not imply that a storm caused damage or that a prior defect has worsened without inspection evidence. Weather-event campaigns should be location- and event-verified.

**When to stop / What to track:** Reassessment booked and completed or closed. Track follow-up conversion and the percentage of reminders tied to valid records.

### Loop 8 - Partner Reactivation

**When to start:** A property manager, realtor, builder, restoration partner, or other referral source sends a completed project or becomes inactive.

**What to do:**


1. Thank the partner after the first completed referral and recognize repeat or annual volume milestones.

2. Keep active partners informed with scope boundaries, documentation examples, response expectations, and the right contact for urgent property issues.

3. At the agreed inactivity threshold, create an internal alert and make a personal check-in to ask about upcoming property needs and service experience.


**Keep in mind:** Do not make insurance-coverage promises or pay/referral claims that have not been approved. Record which partner referred each lead and job.

**When to stop / What to track:** Partner sends another qualified project or remains in occasional helpful partner updates. Track partner-sourced estimates, booked projects, concentration, and reactivation rate.


## General Contracting

The general-contracting journey is driven by project discovery, estimate and scope approval, scheduling and site readiness, project milestones, change orders, closeout documents, warranty records, and repeat work from owners and professional partners.

### Loop 1 - Booking-Cart Upsell

**When to start:** A consultation or estimate is being prepared and the customer's objectives, property, and proposed project scope are known.

**What to do:**


1. Present the base project scope in a clear, itemized way.

2. Offer relevant alternates or complementary scope, such as an additional room, finish upgrade, related repair, or coordinated trade work, where the customer has a real need.

3. Show price and schedule implications separately and record approved options before the estimate becomes the accepted scope.


**Keep in mind:** Do not bury optional upgrades inside the base scope or imply they are required. Check subcontractor availability, permits, and project capacity before offering schedule-sensitive additions.

**When to stop / What to track:** Estimate accepted and options recorded. Track average contract value, option acceptance, gross margin, and scope changes after contract.

### Loop 2 - Pre-Service Add-On

**When to start:** A site visit, mobilization date, or project phase is scheduled and the scope is sufficiently defined.

**What to do:**


1. Confirm site access, decision-maker availability, parking, utilities or other agreed site requirements, and any documents or selections due.

2. Review whether a related item can be included before the phase begins without disrupting the schedule.

3. Record customer approvals and communicate the current scope, start window, and relevant preparation.


**Keep in mind:** Changes to contract scope, price, or schedule require your formal approval process. Do not treat an automated reply as approval for a material change unless the contract process allows it.

**When to stop / What to track:** Phase starts or is rescheduled and approved scope is current. Track site readiness, change-order cycle time, and delay due to missing selections/access.

### Loop 3 - Delivery and Reputation

**When to start:** A defined project milestone or final completion status is confirmed and the corresponding handover materials are ready.

**What to do:**


1. Send a milestone or closeout summary with photos, completed scope, open items, and the next project step.

2. At final handover, provide applicable manuals, warranties, completion documents, care guidance, and payment information.

3. After punch-list items are resolved or clearly assigned, invite honest feedback and a review; create an owned follow-up for any concern.


**Keep in mind:** Do not describe the whole project as complete when only a phase is complete. Clearly distinguish outstanding punch-list or warranty items from closed work.

**When to stop / What to track:** Milestone acknowledged or final closeout completed. Track milestone acceptance, punch-list closure, customer feedback, and review volume.

### Loop 4 - Post-Service Cross-Sell

**When to start:** A project is closed and the customer reaches a suitable follow-up window, or the project record identifies another phase or property need.

**What to do:**


1. Review completed scope and any deferred work the customer explicitly asked to revisit.

2. Send a project-specific check-in and suggest one relevant next phase or complementary service.

3. Offer a consultation or estimate and link the new opportunity to the original project history.


**Keep in mind:** Do not presume that deferred work is still wanted or that a new defect exists. Respect any agreed defect-liability or warranty process and route issues before offering new work.

**When to stop / What to track:** New estimate or phase booked, customer declines, or relationship moves to stay-in-touch messages. Track repeat project rate and revenue per customer/property.

### Loop 5 - Warranty Lifecycle

**When to start:** A documented workmanship, material, or product warranty reaches a set pre-expiry or review milestone.

**What to do:**


1. Use the contract, completion date, product details, and actual warranty schedule to identify the relevant milestone.

2. Send a factual reminder and offer a suitable inspection or service review if your company is qualified and the contract permits it.

3. Route defect claims, coverage questions, or disputed scope to the project manager and responsible warranty contact.


**Keep in mind:** Do not state that an item is covered or excluded without reviewing the applicable documents. Do not use automated messages to decide liability or whether warranty coverage applies.

**When to stop / What to track:** Customer requests a review or the milestone passes. Track warranty requests, response time, completed warranty work, and record completeness.

### Loop 6 - Annual Checkup

**When to start:** A past customer reaches a customer-specific annual property review or a new maintenance/planning cycle.

**What to do:**


1. Use the original project type, asset/property records, and customer preferences to select a relevant check-in.

2. Offer a defined property, exterior, or completed-work review, or a planning consultation for a future phase.

3. Record the new request or place the customer into low-frequency project education if they are not ready.


**Keep in mind:** Do not promise that a periodic visit is included in the original contract unless the contract confirms it. Define the checkup scope and price before booking.

**When to stop / What to track:** Consultation/checkup booked or customer continues to receive occasional, relevant updates. Track repeat project opportunities, annual check-in conversion, and customer lifetime revenue.

### Loop 7 - Re-Test Cycle

**When to start:** A prior repair, inspection finding, installation, or project milestone has a documented follow-up or verification requirement.

**What to do:**


1. Store the original issue or installation, the agreed follow-up condition, and the date or event that should prompt review.

2. Send a factual reminder tied to that record and explain what the follow-up visit is intended to verify.

3. Schedule a qualified site visit and close the follow-up only after findings or required documentation are recorded.


**Keep in mind:** Only create a re-test or verification cycle when the contract, product guidance, project record, or qualified professional supports it. Do not invent a standard interval.

**When to stop / What to track:** Follow-up completed or escalated. Track completion of promised follow-ups, time to verify, and repeat issues.

### Loop 8 - Partner Reactivation

**When to start:** An architect, designer, realtor, property manager, builder, subcontractor, or other approved source refers a project or becomes inactive.

**What to do:**


1. Recognize the first completed project referral and repeat/annual milestones with a personal note or call.

2. Keep active partners informed with capability sheets, project examples, scope boundaries, schedule expectations, and the right project contact.

3. Create an inactivity reminder when activity falls below the partner's normal cycle, then personally ask about upcoming projects and whether the handoff experience can improve.


**Keep in mind:** Keep referral relationships transparent and compliant with contract and industry requirements. Do not promise capacity or pricing without an operational review.

**When to stop / What to track:** Partner refers a qualified new project or continues to receive occasional, relevant updates. Track active partners, qualified opportunities, win rate, partner concentration, and recovered relationships.


# Sample End-to-End Recipes

Use these recipes as starting points. Adjust the service details and timing to fit your trade, appointment length, and normal customer experience.

## Recipe A - The Unbooked Inquiry Follow-Up Loop (Five Steps)

<small>Bonus: a complete five-step follow-up sequence for inquiries that have not yet become bookings.</small>

**Goal:** Make sure every new inquiry receives a prompt, helpful response and a clear opportunity to book.

| Step | When | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Acknowledge** | Immediately | Send a short text or email confirming that the request has been received. Mention the service if known and explain that someone will follow up. |
| **2. Call** | As soon as possible, ideally within 5 minutes during business hours | Call the customer. Ask what they need, answer basic questions, and offer the next step: a booking, site visit, estimate, or scheduled callback. If there is no answer, leave a brief voicemail where appropriate. |
| **3. Helpful message** | About 2 hours later, during reasonable contact hours | If the customer has not replied or booked, send a short text with a simple next step, such as replying with a question or choosing an appointment time. |
| **4. Try again** | Next business day | Make a second call attempt. If the customer is unavailable, ask them to reply with a better time or use the booking link. If they request a later call, follow up on the agreed date. |
| **5. Close politely** | Day 3-5 | Send one final text or email asking whether they still need help. Make it easy to book or reply. If there is no response, stop the active follow-up rather than sending repeated messages. |

**When the customer responds:** Continue the conversation and help them book or agree on a specific next step. Stop the default sequence once a person is actively helping them.

**When the customer books:** Stop the unbooked follow-up and move to the booking and pre-service steps in Loops 1 and 2.

**When the customer declines or asks not to be contacted:** Respect the request and stop the follow-up.

**Important:** Urgent situations, safety concerns, technical questions, complaints, and unusual pricing requests should be handled by a qualified person. Do not promise a price, diagnosis, arrival time, or availability until it has been confirmed.

## Recipe B - From Inquiry to Estimate to Booked Job

**Goal:** Help a suitable prospect move from first contact to a clear estimate and confirmed booking.

1. **Respond:** Acknowledge the request and contact the customer promptly.
2. **Understand the job:** Ask what service is needed, where the work is located, how urgent it is, and whether an estimate or direct booking is the right next step.
3. **Arrange the next step:** Book the service, schedule a site visit, or arrange an estimate. Explain what the customer should expect.
4. **Follow up:** If the customer has not accepted the estimate, check whether they received it and offer to answer questions. Stop when they accept, decline, or ask you to stop.
5. **Confirm the booking:** Record the agreed work, price or estimate, date, address, and any accepted add-ons. Send a confirmation to the customer.

**What to track:** How quickly inquiries receive a reply, how many become bookings, how many estimates are accepted, and the average value of booked work.

## Recipe C - From Booking to a Service-Ready Appointment

**Goal:** Help the customer and your team feel prepared before the visit.

1. Confirm the appointment date, time window, address, and agreed service.
2. Check whether the customer has already accepted or declined relevant add-ons.
3. Before the visit, send a reminder with access details and any approved preparation instructions.
4. Offer one additional service only when it is relevant and can be completed during the same visit.
5. Update the appointment if the customer accepts an extra or requests a change.
6. Send a final reminder using the latest appointment details.

**What to track:** Appointment confirmations, reschedules, missed visits, and additional services booked before arrival.

## Recipe D - From Completed Job to Handover and Review

**Goal:** Make the end of the job as professional as the start.

1. Confirm that the service or project milestone is complete.
2. Make sure the report, job summary, photos, invoice, or project documents are ready.
3. Send the customer the correct information and explain how to ask questions.
4. Check whether there is an open complaint, callback, or outstanding item. Arrange follow-up before sending unrelated promotional messages.
5. Ask for honest feedback and invite the customer to leave a review.

**What to track:** How quickly customers receive their handover, feedback received, reviews, and time taken to resolve issues.

## Recipe E - From First Job to an Ongoing Customer Relationship

**Goal:** Stay useful after the first job and create natural opportunities for repeat business.

1. After the job, identify one helpful tip or piece of information related to the customer's service, property, equipment, or project.
2. When a relevant need arises, offer one related service that your company provides.
3. Use the actual warranty dates to send a reminder when a warranty milestone is approaching.
4. Invite the customer to an annual checkup only when it fits the service history or maintenance guidance.
5. Remind the customer about repeat tests only when the previous test record supports a follow-up date.
6. When the customer books again, confirm the new service and return to the booking steps.

**What to track:** Repeat bookings, revenue from returning customers, and bookings generated by warranty, annual, or re-test reminders.

## Recipe F - From First Referral to a Strong Partner Relationship

**Goal:** Make referral partners feel appreciated and give them a reason to keep working with you.

1. Thank the partner after the first completed referral.
2. Recognize meaningful repeat-business milestones with a personal message or call.
3. Stay in touch with useful updates about services, scheduling, response times, or changes that help the partner serve their customers.
4. When referrals slow down, reach out personally and ask whether anything could be improved.
5. When the partner sends work again, thank them and make sure the experience went smoothly.

**What to track:** Active referral partners, jobs received from partners, repeat referrals, and partners who return after a quiet period.

# Keep Track of What Works

You do not need a complicated dashboard to understand whether these loops are helping. Review a small set of results regularly and focus on completed work and stronger customer relationships - not just the number of messages sent.

| Area | What to check |
|---|---|
| **New inquiries** | How quickly you reply and how many inquiries become booked jobs. |
| **Booking add-ons** | How often customers choose an additional service and how much it adds to the average booking. |
| **Pre-service follow-up** | Appointment confirmations, changes, missed visits, and add-ons booked before arrival. |
| **Customer handover** | Whether customers receive their report or job information promptly, and whether issues are resolved. |
| **Repeat business** | How many past customers book another service and how much revenue comes from returning customers. |
| **Warranty and re-test reminders** | How many relevant reminders lead to a conversation or completed booking. |
| **Referral partners** | How many partners continue to refer work and how many return after a quiet period. |

## A Simple Monthly Review

Once a month, ask:

1. Which loop generated the most booked work or repeat revenue?
2. Where are customers dropping out or waiting too long for a response?
3. Are any messages being sent at the wrong time or to the wrong customers?
4. Which one improvement would make next month’s customer experience better?

Make one or two practical changes, then check whether the results improve. Keep the loops helpful, timely, and easy for customers to act on.
